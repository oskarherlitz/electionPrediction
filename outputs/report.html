Why the picks look like this

Every pick maximizes expected points, q·reward(p), with reward(p)=-log2(max(0.01,p)). Each dot is one race: its Nov-1 price p against the expected points we book. Cheap races (left) pay more — but only if the pick wins, which is why we don't chase the deepest longshots.

The trap we avoided

Read literally, "maximize expected points" bets the underdog everywhere. On FiveThirtyEight's 2018 final forecasts under this exact rule, that realized 23 points versus 41 for favorites — deep longshots barely hit. So our picks cap longshot bets at a forecast floor of q ≥ 0.15.

Method & caveats

  • p — Kalshi yes-bid/ask midpoint, last hourly candle ≤ Nov-1 23:59 ET. Reconstructed, not the organizers' official pull.
  • q — normalized latest pre-election Kalshi price (more informed; distinct from p).
  • Selection — argmax q·reward(p) with a 0.15 underdog floor; same rule both tracks.
  • Coverage — 460/470 priced; 10 flagged fallbacks (KY-SEN + 9 CA same-party seats).
  • Caveat — the floor is tuned on one low-upset cycle; no fundamentals blend (disclosed).
